\section{The local exchange-rate bound and the preorder on the Pauli-plus-erasure class}\label{sec:exchange}

The main line of this section: \emph{discarding an erasure flag is a free operation} (Corollary~\ref{cor:discard}), which gives a rigorous upper bound on the exchange rate (Theorem~\ref{thm:rate}),
and in the simplest subclass $\Nn_{\rm cc}(p,e)$ we completely characterize the preorder induced by local free operations (Theorem~\ref{thm:preorder}).
The gap between the true exchange rate and the bound is one of the core research questions of the project (\S\ref{sec:gap}).

\subsection{Partial flag discarding and the exchange-rate bound}

\begin{theorem}[Partial flag-discarding inequality]\label{thm:partial}
Let $0\le p\le\tfrac34$, $0\le e_0<1$, $0\le\delta\le1-e_0$. For every code distance $d$,
\[
\err_d\big(\Nn_{\rm cc}(p,\,e_0+\delta)\big)\;\le\;\err_d\big(\Nn_{\rm cc}(p'',\,e_0)\big),\qquad
p''=p+\delta\,\frac{\tfrac34-p}{1-e_0}.
\]
Consequently $\alpha_\pm(p,e_0+\delta)\ge\alpha_\pm(p'',e_0)$.\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:basic-ops}(c) with $e=e_0+\delta$ and $\kappa=\delta/(e_0+\delta)$ (trivial if $e_0+\delta=0$).
Then $e'=e(1-\kappa)=e_0$ and $e\kappa=\delta$, so
\[
p'=\frac{(1-e_0-\delta)p+\tfrac34\delta}{1-e_0}=p+\delta\frac{\tfrac34-p}{1-e_0}=p''.
\]
This operation is (F1$'$), so Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone} gives $\err(p,e_0+\delta)\le\err(p'',e_0)$;
the exponent version follows from Corollary~\ref{cor:alpha}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exchange-rate bound]\label{thm:rate}
Let $c(p_0,e_0)=\dfrac{3/4-p_0}{1-e_0}$.

(a) If the right derivatives of $\err_d$ with respect to $p$ and $e$ exist at $(p_0,e_0)$, then
\[\partial_e\err_d\le c\,\partial_p\err_d,\]
and when $\partial_p\err_d>0$, $R^{(d)}:=\partial_e\err_d/\partial_p\err_d\le c(p_0,e_0)$.

(b) Suppose the limit $\alpha=\alpha_-=\alpha_+$ exists, has right derivatives with respect to $p$ and $e$ at $(p_0,e_0)$, and $\partial_p\alpha<0$. Then the local exchange rate satisfies
\[
R_{e\to p}(p_0,e_0)=\frac{\partial_e\alpha}{\partial_p\alpha}=\frac{-\partial_e\alpha}{-\partial_p\alpha}\;\le\;\frac{3/4-p_0}{1-e_0}.
\]
\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In Theorem~\ref{thm:partial}, $p''=p_0+c\,\delta$ is linear in $\delta$. (a): subtract $\err_d(p_0,e_0)$ from both sides and divide by $\delta\downarrow0$;
the left side tends to $\partial_e\err_d$ and the right side to $c\,\partial_p\err_d$. (b): the same for $\alpha$ gives
$\partial_e\alpha\ge c\,\partial_p\alpha$; since $-\partial_p\alpha>0$, multiplying by $-1$ gives
$-\partial_e\alpha\le c(-\partial_p\alpha)$, and dividing by $-\partial_p\alpha>0$ gives $\partial_e\alpha/\partial_p\alpha\le c$. (Both derivatives of $\alpha$ are negative, so $R_{e\to p}>0$; an earlier version of the statement carried a spurious minus sign in front of the ratio, which the proof did not use.)
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Constitutional reading.}
$R_{e\to p}$ here is the \emph{marginal rate of a monotone} (\S\ref{sec:rates}); by Proposition~\ref{prop:marginal} every monotone of the local free order has marginal rate in $[0,c]$, and $c$ is attained by the free-order monotone $1-\mu$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:rate} cannot be improved using the free operations alone.

\begin{remark}[Relation to the heuristic ``$R\le3/4$'']\label{rem:34}
``Adding $\delta e$ of erasure $\to$ adding $\tfrac34\delta e$ of depolarizing noise'' is the value of $c$ at $(p_0,e_0)=(0,0)$. The exact $c$ has two corrections:
(i) the numerator $\tfrac34-p_0$: a newly erased qubit already carried depolarizing noise of probability $p_0$, so the net increase of the nontrivial-Pauli probability is $\tfrac34-p_0$;
(ii) the denominator $1-e_0$: $p$ is the conditional rate on \emph{non-erased qubits}, and the overall nontrivial-Pauli probability is $(1-e)p+\tfrac34 e$.

Hence $c\le\tfrac34$ if and only if $p_0\ge\tfrac34e_0$. At $e_0=0$ we always have $c=\tfrac34-p_0<\tfrac34$;
but at the work point $(p_0,e_0)=(0.02,0.05)$ suggested in the README, $c=0.768>\tfrac34$.
So ``$R\le3/4$'' is a theorem only in the region $p_0\ge\tfrac34e_0$; in general one should use $c(p_0,e_0)$.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Integral form: the ``Pauli equivalent'' of erasure]\label{cor:worth}
For every $(p,e)$ define the equivalent $p_{\rm eq}^{(d)}(p,e):=\inf\{p'\in[0,\tfrac34]:\err_d(p',0)\ge\err_d(p,e)\}$. Then
\[
p_{\rm eq}^{(d)}(p,e)\;\le\;p+e\,(\tfrac34-p),
\]
which is Theorem~\ref{thm:partial} with $e_0=0$ and $\delta=e$. If $\err_d(\cdot,0)$ is strictly increasing there is also the lower bound $p_{\rm eq}^{(d)}\ge p$
(monotonicity under superposed erasure, Proposition~\ref{prop:rate-mono}); the Pauli equivalent of the erasure is then sandwiched between $0$ and $e(\tfrac34-p)$,
that is, $0\le R_{\rm eff}\le\tfrac34-p$.\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{corollary}

\begin{observation}[{Sharpness of the bound: the $[[5,1,3]]$ code attains equality at $e_0=0$}]\label{obs:sharp}
\texttt{theory/checks/exact\_small\_codes.py} enumerates all $4^n$ Pauli errors exactly for three codes with $n\le9$, computes the maximum-likelihood
$\err(p,e)$ in each case, and verifies Theorem~\ref{thm:partial} (all checks pass; see \S\ref{sec:map}). In particular:
\begin{itemize}
\item For the five-qubit code $[[5,1,3]]$ at $e_0=0$ and $p\in\{0.005,0.02,0.06,0.1,0.2,0.3,0.45\}$,
$R^{(d)}=\partial_e\err/\partial_p\err$ and $c=\tfrac34-p$ are \emph{equal} to 8 significant digits.
\item For the Steane code $R^{(d)}/c$ lies between $0.67$ and $0.86$; for the $d=3$ rotated surface code at $p=0.02$, $e_0=0$ one finds $R^{(3)}\approx0.54$ while $c=0.73$.
\end{itemize}
Explanation (now proved for $[[5,1,3]]$, Proposition~\ref{prop:five-sharp}; the general criterion is Theorem~\ref{thm:equality}): for this code, flagging a single qubit does not change the ML decision on any syndrome, so $\err$ is linear along the segment ``replace the law of qubit $j$ from
$\Dep_p$ by $\Dep_{3/4}$'', and the first-order coefficient is exactly $(\tfrac34-p)\partial_{p_j}\err$.
This shows that the inequality $\partial_e\err_d\le c\,\partial_p\err_d$ cannot be improved uniformly over all codes; the strict gap on the surface code is \emph{code dependent}.
\hfill\st{NUMERICAL}; the equality for $[[5,1,3]]$ is now \st{PROVED} for all $0<p<\tfrac34$ (Propositions~\ref{prop:five-sharp}, \ref{prop:five-hand})
\end{observation}

\subsection{Complete characterization of the local-free-operation preorder on the code-capacity class}

\begin{definition}[Local free operations]\label{def:local}
On the i.i.d.\ class, a \emph{local free operation} acts qubit by qubit independently: for each qubit the processor draws $(n,f')$ from a Markov kernel
$Q(n,f'\mid f)$ (with $f\in\{0,1\}$ the input flag, $n\in\F^2$ the superposed Pauli, and $f'\in\{0,1\}$ the output flag),
and sets $E'_j=E_j+n$, $F'_j=f'$. ($n$ may depend on $f$, $f'$, and the processor's randomness, but not on $E_j$; this is the general local form of (F1$'$), (F2), and (F2$'$).)
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Preorder theorem: the Pauli-plus-erasure class]\label{thm:preorder}
Let $p,p'\in[0,\tfrac34)$ and $e,e'\in[0,1)$. The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item there is a local free operation turning $\Nn_{\rm cc}(p,e)$ into $\Nn_{\rm cc}(p',e')$;
\item either $e'\ge e$ and $\lambda(p')\le\lambda(p)$ (that is, $p'\ge p$); or $e'<e$ and $\mu(p',e')\le\mu(p,e)$,
that is, $(1-e')\lambda(p')\le(1-e)\lambda(p)$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, (i) implies $\err_d(p',e')\ge\err_d(p,e)$ for every code and every $d$.\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{(ii)$\Rightarrow$(i).} Case $e'\ge e$, $p'\ge p$: first superpose erasure by Lemma~\ref{lem:basic-ops}(b) to take $e\to e'$,
then superpose depolarizing noise by (a) to take $\lambda\to\lambda(p')$ (this needs $\lambda(q)=\lambda(p')/\lambda(p)\in[0,1]$).
Case $e'<e$, $\mu'\le\mu$: by (c) take $\kappa=1-e'/e$, which gives erasure rate $e'$ and
$\lambda_c=(1-e)\lambda(p)/(1-e')$. The condition $\mu'\le\mu$ is exactly $\lambda(p')\le\lambda_c$, and then (a) superposes the remaining depolarizing noise.

\emph{(i)$\Rightarrow$(ii).} For $P\in\F^2\setminus\{0\}$ write $\hat\nu(P)=\sum_a\nu(a)(-1)^{\langle P,a\rangle}$.
Input: with flag 1 (probability $e$) $E$ is uniform and $\hat E(P)=0$; with flag 0 (probability $1-e$) $\hat E(P)=\lambda:=\lambda(p)$.
Let $q_{f,f'}(n)=Q(n,f'\mid f)$ (a sub-probability measure) with total mass $q_{f,f'}$. The Fourier coefficient of the sub-measure with output flag $1$ is
$(1-e)\lambda\,\hat q_{0,1}(P)$ (the part coming from input flag 1 is uniform and has no nonzero modes).
The output class requires ``$E'$ uniform given flag 1'', so $\lambda\hat q_{0,1}(P)=0$ for all $P\ne0$; since $\lambda>0$, $q_{0,1}$ is a uniform measure.
Let $s_0=\sum_n q_{0,0}(n)$; then $\sum q_{0,1}=1-s_0$, and the probability of output flag $0$ is
\[
m':=1-e'=e\,y+(1-e)s_0,\qquad y:=\textstyle\sum_nq_{1,0}(n)\in[0,1].
\]
The Fourier coefficient at $P\neq0$ of the sub-measure with output flag 0 is $(1-e)\lambda\,\hat q_{0,0}(P)$, and the output class requires it to equal
$m'\lambda'$ (with $\lambda'=\lambda(p')$). Since $|\hat q_{0,0}(P)|\le s_0$,
\[
m'\lambda'\le(1-e)\lambda\,s_0.\tag{$*$}
\]
If $e'\ge e$ (that is, $m'\le1-e$): $m'\ge(1-e)s_0$, and substituting into $(*)$ gives $\lambda'\le\lambda$.
If $e'<e$: $s_0\le1$, and $(*)$ gives $(1-e')\lambda'\le(1-e)\lambda$.

The last sentence follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Level-1 form.}
For the uncoded qubit, (i) and (ii) are also equivalent to the existence of an arbitrary simulable reduction (label permutations beyond translations) and to $\Phi(P')\le\Phi(P)$ for all $\varphi\in\Phi_4$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:local-level1}); the complete family of monotones is described in Theorem~\ref{thm:complete}.

\begin{remark}[Structure]
(1) Complete monotones: the local free order is completely determined by two functionals: the total unflagged eigenvalue mass $\mu=(1-e)\lambda$ (nonincreasing under any local free operation)
and ``the conditional eigenvalue $\lambda$ is nonincreasing when the erasure rate does not decrease''.
(2) Slope of the boundary of the reachable set: the steepest direction for lowering $e'$ starting from $(p,e)$ is $\mu'=\mu$, whose tangent slope is exactly
$dp'/de'=-c(p',e')$, consistent with the $c$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:rate}: $c$ is the boundary slope of the free order.
(3) The order is not total; for example, if $(p,e)$ and $(p',e')$ satisfy $e'<e$ but $\mu'>\mu$, neither direction is reachable.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Hellinger/Chernoff monotones}

\begin{proposition}[Hellinger monotones]\label{prop:hellinger}
For a noise instance $P=P_{EF}$, $G\in V$, and $s\in(0,1)$ let
$\BC_s(P;G)=\sum_{E,F}P(E,F)^{1-s}P(E+G,F)^s$.
If $P'$ is obtained by a free operation of type (F1) or (F2)/(F2$'$), then $\BC_s(P';G)\ge\BC_s(P;G)$ (for the same $G$).\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
These operations are Markov kernels $W$ acting on $(E,F)$ (discarding/coarse-graining flags; superposition of an $E$-independent $N$ is a convolution),
and translation commutes with convolution: $(WP)(\cdot+G)=W(P(\cdot+G))$. The function $(x,y)\mapsto x^{1-s}y^s$ is positively homogeneous and concave, hence superadditive,
$\sum_u(\sum_vW(u|v)x_v)^{1-s}(\sum_vW(u|v)y_v)^s\ge\sum_v x_v^{1-s}y_v^s$. Take $x=P$, $y=P(\cdot+G)$.
\end{proof}

For $\Nn_{\rm cc}(p,e)$ and any nonzero single-qubit shift $G_j\in\{X,Y,Z\}$ (the three are symmetric), the single-qubit coefficient is
\[
b_s(p,e)=e+(1-e)\Big[(1-p)^{1-s}(\tfrac p3)^s+(\tfrac p3)^{1-s}(1-p)^s+\tfrac{2p}3\Big],
\]
where the first term comes from $F=1$ (the uniform distribution is invariant under the shift). Since $b_s=b_{1-s}$ and $b_s$ is convex in $s$, $\min_s b_s=b_{1/2}$. Put
\[
\beta_{\rm dep}(p)=2\sqrt{p(1-p)/3}+2p/3,\qquad B(p,e)=b_{1/2}(p,e)=e+(1-e)\beta_{\rm dep}(p).
\]
$B$ is the \emph{single-qubit Bhattacharyya parameter}. For each $s$, the same argument as in Theorem~\ref{thm:partial} together with Proposition~\ref{prop:hellinger}
(partial flag discarding does not decrease $b_s$) gives $\partial_eb_s\le c\,\partial_pb_s$, that is, the Hellinger exchange rate satisfies
\[
R_s(p,e)=\frac{\partial_eb_s}{\partial_pb_s}\le c(p,e).
\]
Write $R_B:=R_{1/2}=\dfrac{1-\beta_{\rm dep}(p)}{(1-e)\,\beta_{\rm dep}'(p)}$.

\subsection{The gap problem}\label{sec:gap}

The free order $\succeq_{\rm free}$ is a sub-order of the \emph{operational order} (comparing $\err_d$ or $\alpha$ for a given code). Let $R_\alpha$ be the true exchange rate on the surface code.
\begin{problem}[Exchange-rate gap]\label{prob:gap}
Quantify $\Delta:=c-R_\alpha\ge0$, and decide whether the gap comes from
\begin{enumerate}[(A)]
\item \emph{code dependence}: the \emph{code-universal} operational order (requiring $\err$ not to increase for all linear decoding structures and all $n$) coincides with the local free order,
and the $\alpha$-order of the surface code is merely a coarser order; or
\item \emph{incompleteness of the free operations}: there are free operations outside Definition~\ref{def:local} (nonlocal or code dependent) that would make the order finer?
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}

\begin{conjecture}[Code-universal order = local free order]\label{conj:universal}
Within $\Nn_{\rm cc}(p,e)$, the \emph{code-universal finite-$n$ operational order} coincides with the local free order of Theorem~\ref{thm:preorder}.
\hfill\st{CONJECTURE}
\end{conjecture}
\noindent\emph{Reformulation (constitutional audit).} The all-losses version of this conjecture (compare $\Phi(P')\le\Phi(P)$ for all $\varphi\in\Phi_\Lambda$, over all structures) is proved by taking the uncoded qubit (Proposition~\ref{prop:local-level1}, Remark~\ref{rem:conj-reform}); what remains open is the single-loss statement for $\err=1-\Phi_{\max}$. \emph{Status after the search of \S\ref{sec:single-loss}:} it holds (proved) for every unreachable pair with $\mu'>\mu$ or $\nu'>\nu$, in particular whenever $e'<e$; for the remaining ``trade'' pairs the search finds no reversing structure (Observation~\ref{obs:search}), which is evidence that it is false.

\noindent\emph{Evidence and route.} Observation~\ref{obs:sharp} gives first-order evidence at $e_0=0$: the boundary slope $c$ is attained by some code.
Route to a proof: (S1) for each $n$ and linear structure $(\sigma,L)$, the comparison of $\err$ is a Blackwell comparison of the ``experiments'' given by $n$ independent copies
(Blackwell--Sherman--Stein: experiment $A$ is at least as good as $B$ for all priors and losses if and only if $B$ is a randomized processing of $A$ \cite{Blackwell});
(S2) the decision problems arising from linear structures form a proper subclass of all decision problems, and one must show that they suffice to separate the two functionals $\mu$ and $(e,\lambda)$ of the local free order;
(S3) the asymptotic version is characterized, via the large-sample Blackwell order, by the family of all R\'enyi divergences \cite{MPST}, which corresponds exactly to $\{b_s\}_{s\in(0,1)}$ and its higher-order generalizations.
If the conjecture holds, the gap is case (A) and $c-R_\alpha$ measures information specific to the surface code rather than a deficiency of the free operations;
if it is refuted by a counterexample from some code (for example one other than $[[5,1,3]]$), new free operations are needed (case (B)).

\begin{conjecture}[Completeness of $B$: hypothesis $H_B$]\label{conj:HB}
In some neighborhood within $\Nn_{\rm cc}$, the $\alpha(p,e)$ of the surface code is a function of $B(p,e)$, so that $R_\alpha=R_B$.
\hfill\st{CONJECTURE}
\end{conjecture}
\noindent This is testable: the numerics of P1 give $R_\alpha$, to be compared with the analytic $R_B$. $R_\alpha\neq R_B$ would show that
the Bhattacharyya monotone is incomplete, and the next candidates are $b_s$ ($s\ne\tfrac12$). Proposition~\ref{prop:genie} bounds $\alpha$ above by $\ln(1/B)$; the matching lower bound is a function of $B_M$, not of $B$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:sandwich}; the union-bound version is vacuous, Proposition~\ref{prop:lower}(b)); and a sandwich does not constrain derivatives anyway, so $H_B$ needs its own test. What is proved about it is in \S\ref{sec:hb}.

\subsection{Analytic formula for derivatives and the envelope argument}\label{sec:envelope}

The sampling scheme of P1 (README \S2) obtains $\partial_\theta\pL$ directly from one table $f(k,w)$, without decoding at neighboring parameter points. For decoders independent of $(p,e)$ (MWPM) this is an identity;
for the maximum-likelihood decoder it relies on the following argument.

\begin{remark}[Envelope argument for ML derivatives]\label{rem:envelope}
Let $D^\ast$ be the ML decoder at $\theta^\ast=(p^\ast,e^\ast)$ (a fixed map). For a fixed decoder $D$,
$\pL(\theta;D)=\sum_{k,w}P_\theta(k,w)\,f_D(k,w)$, where $f_D$ does not depend on $\theta$. Optimality gives $\err(\theta)=\min_D\pL(\theta;D)\le\pL(\theta;D^\ast)$,
with equality at $\theta^\ast$. The difference $g(\theta)=\pL(\theta;D^\ast)-\err(\theta)\ge0$ satisfies $g(\theta^\ast)=0$, so wherever both are differentiable at $\theta^\ast$ we have $\nabla g(\theta^\ast)=0$, that is,
\[
\partial_\theta\err\big|_{\theta^\ast}=\sum_{k,w}\partial_\theta P_\theta(k,w)\,f_{D^\ast}(k,w).
\]
The ML decoder depends on $\theta$ only through $p$: the erased positions are known, so $e$ never enters the posterior given the flags. Therefore
the $e$-derivative is an exact identity for ML, and the envelope argument is needed only for $\partial_p$.
Hence $\partial_\theta\ln\pL=\langle s_\theta\rangle_{\rm fail}$ (with $s_\theta=\partial_\theta\ln P_\theta$ the score function and the conditional expectation taken over failure events),
and $\partial\alpha/\partial\theta$ is minus the least-squares slope of $\langle s_\theta\rangle_{\rm fail}$ against $d$ (the least-squares slope is linear in the data and commutes with differentiation).
Differentiability: at finite $d$, $\err$ is the minimum of finitely many smooth functions; one-sided derivatives always exist, and they equal the frozen-decoder derivatives when the ML decision is unique (Lemma~\ref{lem:danskin}, which makes this remark rigorous).
\hfill\st{PROVED} (via Lemma~\ref{lem:danskin})
\end{remark}

\begin{observation}[Exact verification of the envelope identity]\label{obs:envelope}
Check C8 of \texttt{exact\_small\_codes.py}: for $[[5,1,3]]$, the Steane code, and the $d=3$ rotated surface code, at $(p^\ast,e^\ast)\in\{(0.04,0.05),(0.1,0.2)\}$,
the derivatives $\partial_p\err,\partial_e\err$ of the exact ML agree with the derivatives of the failure rate of the \emph{frozen decoder} to within relative $2\times10^{-10}$.
In addition, \texttt{experiments/p1\_erasure\_pauli/envelope\_check.py} tests the $p$-derivative at $d=5,7$ with the exact transfer-matrix ML decoder on common random samples
of each stratum $(k,w)$. A central difference has a deterministic $O(h^2)$ discretisation error (about $+0.3\%$ at $d=5$ and $+1.9\%$ at $d=7$ for $h=0.01$, $p=0.06$, growing four times for $2h$),
so the test compares the finite difference with re-matched decoders ($B_{\rm matched}$) against the \emph{same} finite difference with the frozen decoder ($B_{\rm frozen}$).
Their difference is at most $0.04\%$ of the derivative (within $1.6$ bootstrap standard errors) in four cases, one of which has erasure strata; one deviation of $2.05$ standard errors ($0.1\%$ of the derivative) occurs at step $2h$, $d=7$.
At $d=9$ (\texttt{envelope\_check.py --extended}) the test passes for the exact decoder at $e=0$ and $e=0.05$ and for the truncated-MPS decoder ($\chi=8$) at $e=0$, with $|z|\le1.25$ at both steps (\texttt{results/envelope\_check\_extended.json}).\hfill\st{NUMERICAL}
\end{observation}

\subsection{The single-loss form of Conjecture~\ref{conj:universal}: what is proved and what a search shows}\label{sec:single-loss}

A structure $\mathfrak D$ \emph{reverses} a pair $(P,P')$ if $\err_{\mathfrak D}(P')<\err_{\mathfrak D}(P)$. The single-loss form of Conjecture~\ref{conj:universal} says: every pair $(P,P')$ in $\Nn_{\rm cc}$ that is not related by a local free operation is reversed by some linear decoding structure (on any number $n$ of qubits).
By Remark~\ref{rem:conj-reform} the all-losses form is already proved; this subsection concerns $\err$ alone.

\begin{proposition}[The free order is cut out by two scalars]\label{prop:two-scalars}
For $p,p'\in[0,\tfrac34)$, $e,e'\in[0,1)$, condition (ii) of Theorem~\ref{thm:preorder} holds if and only if $p'\ge p$ and $\mu(p',e')\le\mu(p,e)$, where $\mu=(1-e)\lambda(p)$.\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $e'\ge e$, then $p'\ge p$ gives $\lambda'\le\lambda$ and $1-e'\le1-e$, hence $\mu'\le\mu$. If $e'<e$ and $\mu'\le\mu$ then $\lambda'\le\lambda(1-e)/(1-e')<\lambda$, hence $p'>p$. Conversely, if $e'\ge e$ the condition is $p'\ge p$, and if $e'<e$ it is $\mu'\le\mu$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Two structures, and a family of monotones]\label{prop:two-structures}
(a) For the uncoded qubit, $\err=\tfrac34(1-\mu)$. It reverses exactly the pairs with $\mu'>\mu$.
(b) Let $F_2$ be the two-qubit structure with $S=0$, $N=\langle XX,ZZ\rangle$ (syndrome $E_0+E_1$, label $E_0$). Then $\err=p(1-e^2)+\tfrac34e^2=\tfrac34(1-\nu)$ with $\nu=(1-e^2)\lambda(p)$. It reverses exactly the pairs with $\nu'>\nu$.
(c) For every $k\ge1$ the function $\nu_k=(1-e^k)\lambda(p)$ is a monotone of the local free order ($\nu_1=\mu$, $\nu_2=\nu$), and every unreachable pair with $e,e'<1$ is separated by some $\nu_k$: $\nu_k'>\nu_k$.
Consequently the single-loss form holds for every unreachable pair with $\mu'>\mu$ or $\nu'>\nu$; in particular for every unreachable pair with $e'<e$.\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) For $f=0$ the best guess has probability $(1-e)(1-p)$, for $f=1$ it is $e/4$, so $1-\err=(1-e)(1-p)+e/4=\tfrac34\mu+\tfrac14$ (use $1-p=\tfrac34\lambda+\tfrac14$).
(b) Write $\err=\sum_A\Pr[A]\,\err_A$ over the erased set $A\subseteq\{0,1\}$. For $A=\emptyset$: $\max_\ell P_0(\ell)P_0(\ell+s)$ equals $(1-p)^2$ for $s=0$ and $(1-p)p/3$ for each of the three $s\ne0$ (the two labels $\ell\in\{I,s\}$ tie), so $1-\err_\emptyset=(1-p)^2+(1-p)p=1-p$.
For $A=\{0\}$ the maximum over $\ell$ is $\tfrac14(1-p)$ for each of the four syndromes; for $A=\{1\}$ it is $P_0(I)/4=(1-p)/4$; in both cases $\err_A=p$. For $A=\{0,1\}$ all sixteen $(\ell,s)$ have probability $1/16$, so $\err_A=\tfrac34$.
(c) If $e'\ge e$, $p'\ge p$ then $1-e'^k\le1-e^k$ and $\lambda'\le\lambda$. If $e'<e$ and $\mu'\le\mu$, write $\nu_k=\mu\sum_{i<k}e^i$, which is increasing in $e$ and in $\mu$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:two-scalars} these are all reachable pairs. If a pair is unreachable and $e'<e$ then $\mu'>\mu$; if $e'\ge e$ then $p'<p$, and $\nu_k'-\nu_k\to\lambda'-\lambda>0$ as $k\to\infty$.
\end{proof}

The unreachable pairs not covered by Proposition~\ref{prop:two-structures} form the \emph{trade region}: $e'\ge e$, $p'<p$, $\mu'\le\mu$, $\nu'\le\nu$ (less Pauli noise, more erasure). Every $\nu_k$ separates the pairs of this region for large $k$, so the single-loss form would follow from structures that realize the order of $\nu_k$ for arbitrarily large $k$ (``erasure-hardened'' structures with $\err_A\approx p$ for all $|A|<k$). The structures $F_2$ and, for $k=3$ only partially, the three-fold Pauli repetition $\mathrm{Rep}_3$ ($S=0$, $N=\langle XXX,ZZZ\rangle$; $\err_A=p$ for $1\le|A|\le2$, second order in $p$ for $A=\emptyset$, $\tfrac34$ for $|A|=3$) are the only such structures found.

\begin{observation}[Search for reversals of trade pairs]\label{obs:search}
\texttt{checks/universal\_order\_search.py} enumerates \emph{all} linear decoding structures on $n\le3$ qubits (every nested pair $S<N$ of subspaces of $\F^{2n}$: $92{,}881$ at $n=3$, $528$ distinct $\err$ tables) with exact ML, and hill-climbs for $n=4,5$.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item \emph{Marginal rates.} The smallest marginal rate $R_{\mathfrak D}/c$ over all structures on $n\le3$ qubits is $0$ at $e_0=0$ (attained by $F_2$, $\err_A=p$ for $|A|\le1$), equals $2e_0/(1+e_0)$ for $e_0\le0.1$ at the tested points (attained by $F_2$), and equals $0.2617$ at $(p_0,e_0)=(0.0213,0.2)$ (attained by $\mathrm{Rep}_3$; $F_2$ gives $0.3333$). Hill-climbing at $n=4,5$ does not improve on $2e_0/(1+e_0)$ at $e_0=0.05$ and reaches $0.242$ ($n=4$) and $0.237$ ($n=5$) at $e_0=0.2$. For large $k$ the rate of $\mathrm{Rep}_k$ tends to $R_B/c=0.2501$ at $p_0=0.0213$.
\item \emph{First-order wedge.} Just beyond the free-order boundary at $(0.0213,0.2)$, the pair with $\kappa=0.30$ ($P'=(p_0-\kappa c\,\Delta,\,e_0+\Delta)$, $\Delta=0.01$) is reversed by $\mathrm{Rep}_3$ but by neither the uncoded qubit nor $F_2$; the reversed set on $n\le3$ is therefore strictly larger than $\{\mu'>\mu\}\cup\{\nu'>\nu\}$.
\item \emph{Trade pairs.} For the six pairs A, B, D, E, F, G of the script (for example $(0.0213,0.05)\to(0,0.25)$, which has no Pauli noise at all, and $(0.0213,0)\to(0.0150,0.30)$, which starts at $e_0=0$), no structure on $n\le3$ qubits reverses the pair (smallest difference $\err(P')-\err(P)$ between $+2\times10^{-3}$ and $+1.7\times10^{-2}$), and neither does hill-climbing at $n=4,5$ (smallest ratio $\err(P')/\err(P)$: $1.28$ for pair G, which lies closest to the boundary $\nu'=\nu$, and between $1.49$ and $3.66$ for the others).
\end{enumerate}
\hfill\st{NUMERICAL}
\end{observation}

\begin{remark}[Reading of the search]\label{rem:search}
(1) The search found \emph{no} structure that reverses a pair of the trade region beyond what $\mu$, $\nu$ and $\mathrm{Rep}_3$ already do, and the margins are clear (ratios between $1.28$ and $3.66$ in the hill-climbing runs). This is evidence that the single-loss form of Conjecture~\ref{conj:universal} \emph{fails} in the trade region for linear structures, but it is not a proof: $n\ge6$ is not searched, and reversals for large $n$ would be governed by exponents, which are not controlled here.
(2) If the failure persists for all $n$, the code-universal $\err$-order is strictly larger than the free order, while the all-losses order equals it (Proposition~\ref{prop:local-level1}). The extra comparabilities are then a property of the single loss, not evidence of missing free operations; no new free operation is needed for the uncoded theory.
(3) In terms of marginal rates (\S\ref{sec:rates}): $\inf_{\mathfrak D}R_{\mathfrak D}(x_0)$ is $0$ at $e_0=0$ and, in all searches at $p_0\ge0.02$, positive at $e_0>0$, with $c$ attained by the uncoded qubit. Proposition~\ref{prop:rate-small-p} shows that the infimum tends to $0$ as $p_0\to0$ at fixed $e_0$ (large repetition structures), so a universal lower bound on the exchange rate, complementing Theorem~\ref{thm:rate}, cannot be uniform in $p_0$; whether it is positive for each fixed $p_0>0$ is open and is a question about large structures (\S\ref{sec:hardened}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Erasure-hardened structures: obstructions, and what does not follow}\label{sec:hardened}

A structure is \emph{erasure-hardened of order $k$} if its failure probability with erased set $A$ does not exceed its failure probability without erasures, to leading order in $p$, for every $|A|<k$.
The structure $F_2$ is hardened of order $2$ with failure $\err_\emptyset=p$; a hardened family of arbitrary order would settle the single-loss form of Conjecture~\ref{conj:universal} at first order.
Write $V_A$ for the Paulis supported on $A\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}$, $\err_A(p)$ for the ML failure probability with erased set $A$, and $d_K$ for the minimal weight of an element of $N\setminus S$ (the shortest logical operator).

\begin{lemma}[Erasure jump]\label{lem:jump}
If $G\in N\setminus S$ is supported on $T$, then $\err_A(p)\ge\tfrac12$ for every $A\supseteq T$ and every $p$.\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Given $A$, the law $P_A$ of the fault is uniform on the qubits of $A$, so $P_A(E+G)=P_A(E)$ for all $E$. Since $G\in N=\ker\sigma$ the syndrome is unchanged, and since $G\notin S$ the class $E+S$ is mapped to a different class $E+G+S$ of the same syndrome with the same weight.
Hence for every syndrome $s$ the best class $c^\ast$ has a twin $c^\ast+G\neq c^\ast$ of equal weight, so $\max_cW(c)\le\tfrac12\sum_{c'}W(c')$; summing over $s$ gives success probability $\le\tfrac12$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Erasure-only failure]\label{lem:erasure-only}
$\err_A(0)=1-2^{-r(A)}$ with $r(A)=\dim(N\cap V_A)-\dim(S\cap V_A)$ (the number of independent logical operators supported on $A$). Hence the erasure-only failure of any structure is $\err(0,e)=\mathbb E_A\big[1-2^{-r(A)}\big]$.\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At $p=0$ the fault is uniform on $V_A$. Its syndromes are the cosets of $N\cap V_A$ in $V_A$; inside one syndrome the classes are the cosets of $S\cap V_A$, all of the same size, so there are $2^{r(A)}$ equally likely classes and the best guess succeeds with probability $2^{-r(A)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Failure of order $j$ needs a logical operator of weight $\le2j$]\label{lem:first-order}
If $\err_\emptyset(p)/p^j\not\to0$ as $p\to0$, then $d_K\le2j$. In particular, if $\err_\emptyset(p)/p\not\to0$ then $d_K\le2$.\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $d_K>2j$. Two faults of weight $\le j$ with the same syndrome differ by an element of $N$ of weight $\le2j$, which must lie in $S$; so all faults of weight $\le j$ with a given syndrome lie in one class $c_0$, and every other class of that syndrome contains only faults of weight $>j$, hence has weight $\le Cp^{j+1}$, while $W(c_0)\ge(p/3)^j(1-p)^n$. For small $p$ the class $c_0$ wins, so only faults of weight $>j$ cause failures and $\err_\emptyset=O(p^{j+1})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Hardening beyond order $2j$ is impossible]\label{cor:hardening}
If $\err_\emptyset(p)=\Theta(p^j)$, there is $A$ with $|A|\le2j$ and $\err_A\ge\tfrac12$, so $\err(p,e)\ge\tfrac12e^{2j}$. A structure that fails at first order in $p$ cannot be erasure-hardened of any order $\ge3$: some pair of erasures already costs at least $\tfrac12$. In particular there is no structure with $\err_A(p)=\Theta(p)$ for all $|A|\le2$.\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[The marginal rate at small $p$ is not bounded below]\label{prop:rate-small-p}
For the Pauli repetition code $\mathrm{Rep}_k$ ($k$ copies of the Pauli, $S=0$, $N=\langle X^{\otimes k},Z^{\otimes k}\rangle$; $\mathrm{Rep}_1$ is the uncoded qubit and $\mathrm{Rep}_2=F_2$),
\[
\lim_{p\to0}\frac{R_{\mathrm{Rep}_k}(p,e)}{c(p,e)}=\frac{2e}{2e+(k-1)(1-e)}\qquad(0<e<1).
\]
Consequently, for every $e_0\in(0,1)$ and $\delta>0$ there is $p_\delta>0$ such that for all $p_0<p_\delta$ some structure has $R/c<\delta$: $r_{\min}(p_0,e_0)/c\to0$ as $p_0\to0$, so no lower bound on the marginal rate can be uniform in $p_0$.\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
With $m$ non-erased copies the failure is $\err_m=\tfrac34$ for $m=0$, $\err_1=\err_2=p$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:two-structures} and the single-copy case), and $\err_m=O(p^2)$ for $m\ge3$ (every single copy error is corrected by the majority). Hence
$\err(p,e)=\sum_m\binom km(1-e)^me^{k-m}\err_m(p)$ gives $\partial_e\err(0,e)=\tfrac34ke^{k-1}$ and
$\partial_p\err(0,e)=k(1-e)e^{k-1}+\binom k2(1-e)^2e^{k-2}$; divide by $c(0,e)=\tfrac34/(1-e)$. For fixed $k$ the limit $p\to0$ is attained continuously, and the right side tends to $0$ as $k\to\infty$.
\end{proof}

\begin{observation}[Checks of Lemmas~\ref{lem:jump}--\ref{lem:first-order} and of the small-$p$ rate]\label{obs:hardened}
\texttt{checks/hardened\_structures\_search.py} verifies Lemma~\ref{lem:jump} ($\err_A\ge\tfrac12$ on the support of a minimum-weight logical), Lemma~\ref{lem:erasure-only} (to $10^{-12}$) and Lemma~\ref{lem:first-order} ($j=1$) on \emph{every} linear structure on $n\le3$ qubits ($92{,}881$ at $n=3$); the smallest marginal rate over these structures at $p_0=10^{-4}$ is $0.0523\,c$ at $e_0=0.05$ and $0.2003\,c$ at $e_0=0.2$ (attained by $\mathrm{Rep}_3$, $R/c\to e_0$), against $2e_0/(1+e_0)=0.095,\ 0.333$ for $F_2$. At the moderate rate $p_0=0.0213$, hill-climbing at $n=6$ (about $1.3\times10^4$ evaluations per point) finds nothing below $F_2$ at $e_0=0.05$ ($0.0952\,c$) and reaches $0.2352\,c$ at $e_0=0.2$ ($0.2419\,c$ at $n=4$, $0.2365\,c$ at $n=5$): no erasure-hardened structure of order $3$ appears, as Corollary~\ref{cor:hardening} predicts for structures that fail at first order.\hfill\st{NUMERICAL}
\end{observation}

\begin{remark}[Large structures and channel-coding exponents]\label{rem:exponents}
For $S=0$ a structure with $2^q$ labels is a linear code of rate $R=q\ln2/n$ on the additive Pauli channel with erasure flags known to the decoder, and $\err$ is its ML decoding error. Gallager's random-coding and expurgated exponents $E_r,E_{\rm ex}$ are achieved by random linear codes and the sphere-packing exponent $E_{\rm sp}$ bounds every code from below (see \texttt{checks/exponent\_criterion.py} for the formulas).
So if $\max_R[\max(E_r,E_{\rm ex})(R;P')-E_{\rm sp}(R;P)]>0$, large structures reverse the pair $(P,P')$. This criterion is \emph{not} met for any of the six trade pairs A, B, D, E, F, G of Observation~\ref{obs:search} (the maximal gap is negative, between $-0.006$ and $-0.11$ nats, attained at the highest rates), and for $R\to0$ the Bhattacharyya parameter $B$ of $P'$ exceeds that of $P$ in every case.
This strengthens the evidence of Remark~\ref{rem:search}, but only for non-degenerate ($S=0$) structures: the sphere-packing converse does not cover degenerate structures, and the criterion is sufficient for a reversal, not necessary.
It also shows what a decisive analysis needs: the order of the whole family of exponent curves $R\mapsto E(R;P)$, not a single Rényi parameter.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Concavity, the envelope identity, and equality in the exchange-rate bound}\label{sec:equality}

This subsection gives a proof of the envelope argument (Remark~\ref{rem:envelope}), a form of Theorem~\ref{thm:rate}(a) that needs no differentiability assumption, and a characterization of the codes and work points at which the bound is attained (open problem O6, Observation~\ref{obs:sharp}).
Fix a linear decoding structure on $n$ qubits. For an erased set $A$ and rates $q=(q_j)_{j\notin A}\in[0,\tfrac34]^{n-|A|}$ let $\err_A(q)$ be the ML error when the qubits of $A$ are erased (flags known) and qubit $j\notin A$ carries $\Dep_{q_j}$; write $\err_A(p)$ when all $q_j=p$.
A \emph{decision rule} $D$ maps each syndrome to a class; $L_A(q;D)$ is its failure probability, so $\err_A(q)=\min_DL_A(q;D)$ over the finite set of rules, and $\mathcal D^\ast_A(q)$ is the set of minimizers (the ML rules).

\begin{lemma}[One qubit: affine failure rates, concave ML error, and the flag at rate $\tfrac34$]\label{lem:concave}
Fix $A$, $j\notin A$ and the rates of the other qubits.
(a) $q_j\mapsto L_A(q;D)$ is affine on $[0,\tfrac34]$ for every $D$, and $q_j\mapsto\err_A(q)$ is concave.
(b) At $q_j=\tfrac34$, $\err_A(q)=\err_{A\cup\{j\}}(q_{-j})$: a qubit at rate $\tfrac34$ is the same decision problem as an erased qubit.
\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(a) $\Dep_{q_j}=(1-q_j)\delta_I+\tfrac{q_j}3(\delta_X+\delta_Y+\delta_Z)$ is affine in $q_j$, hence so is the product law, every class weight $W_c(q)$ and $L_A(q;D)=1-\sum_sW_{D(s)}(q)$.
$\err_A=1-\sum_s\max_cW_c$ is one minus a sum of maxima of affine functions, hence concave. (b) $\Dep_{3/4}$ is the uniform law; given $A$ the position $j$ is fixed, so its flag carries no information beyond the law, and both problems have the same joint law of class and syndrome.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Danskin form of the envelope identity]\label{lem:danskin}
Let $\theta\mapsto P_\theta$ be a family of noise laws on a structure such that every $L(\theta;D)$ is continuous near $\theta^\ast$ and has a one-sided directional derivative $\partial_vL(\theta^\ast;D)$ in a direction $v$. Then $\err(\theta)=\min_DL(\theta;D)$ has the one-sided directional derivative
\[
\partial_v^+\err(\theta^\ast)=\min_{D\in\mathcal D^\ast(\theta^\ast)}\partial_vL(\theta^\ast;D).
\]
In particular, if all ML rules at $\theta^\ast$ have the same derivative (for example, if the ML class is unique for every syndrome), the derivative of the ML error equals the derivative of the failure rate of the decoder frozen at $\theta^\ast$.\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
There are finitely many rules. A rule $D\notin\mathcal D^\ast$ has $L(\theta^\ast;D)>\err(\theta^\ast)$, so by continuity it is not a minimizer for $\theta=\theta^\ast+hv$ with $h>0$ small. Hence $\err(\theta^\ast+hv)=\min_{D\in\mathcal D^\ast}\big[\err(\theta^\ast)+h\,\partial_vL(\theta^\ast;D)+o(h)\big]=\err(\theta^\ast)+h\min_{D\in\mathcal D^\ast}\partial_vL(\theta^\ast;D)+o(h)$.
\end{proof}

\noindent Lemma~\ref{lem:danskin} applies to $\Nn_{\rm cc}(p,e)$ in every direction ($L$ is a polynomial in $p$ and $e$), and it upgrades Remark~\ref{rem:envelope} to a theorem: the frozen-decoder derivative used by the P1 estimator is the derivative of $\err$ whenever the ML decision is unique, and in general it bounds the right derivative from above (the frozen rule is one of the minimizers). The $e$-derivative needs no envelope argument (Remark~\ref{rem:envelope}).

\begin{theorem}[Exchange-rate bound with one-sided derivatives]\label{thm:rate-right}
For every linear decoding structure and every $0\le p<\tfrac34$, $0\le e<1$,
\[
\partial_e\err(p,e)\;\le\;c(p,e)\,\partial_p^+\err(p,e),\qquad c=\frac{3/4-p}{1-e},
\]
where $\partial_e\err$ exists ($\err$ is a polynomial in $e$) and $\partial_p^+$ is the right derivative, which exists by Lemma~\ref{lem:danskin}. No differentiability assumption is needed.\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $\pi_A=e^{|A|}(1-e)^{n-|A|}$, so $\err(p,e)=\sum_A\pi_A\err_A(p)$.
(1) Reindexing $B=A\cup\{j\}$ and using $\pi_{B\setminus\{j\}}=\pi_B(1-e)/e$ gives
$\partial_e\err=\sum_A\pi_A\big(\tfrac{|A|}e-\tfrac{n-|A|}{1-e}\big)\err_A=\tfrac1{1-e}\sum_A\pi_A\sum_{j\notin A}\big[\err_{A\cup\{j\}}(p)-\err_A(p)\big]$ (for $e=0$ only $A=\emptyset$ contributes).
(2) Let $f_{A,j}(q)$ be $\err_A$ with qubit $j$ at rate $q$ and the others at $p$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:concave}, $\err_{A\cup\{j\}}(p)-\err_A(p)=f_{A,j}(\tfrac34)-f_{A,j}(p)\le(\tfrac34-p)\,f_{A,j}'^{\,+}(p)$ (a concave function lies below its right tangent).
(3) By Lemma~\ref{lem:danskin}, $f_{A,j}'^{\,+}(p)=\min_{D\in\mathcal D^\ast_A}\partial_{q_j}L_A(p;D)$ and $\partial_p^+\err_A(p)=\min_{D\in\mathcal D^\ast_A}\sum_{j\notin A}\partial_{q_j}L_A(p;D)\ge\sum_{j\notin A}f_{A,j}'^{\,+}(p)$.
Combining, $\partial_e\err\le\tfrac{3/4-p}{1-e}\sum_A\pi_A\sum_{j\notin A}f_{A,j}'^{\,+}(p)\le c\sum_A\pi_A\,\partial_p^+\err_A(p)=c\,\partial_p^+\err$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[When the bound is attained]\label{thm:equality}
Let $0<p<\tfrac34$, $0\le e<1$. Then $\partial_e\err=c\,\partial_p^+\err$ if and only if, for every erased set $A$ with $\pi_A>0$ (only $A=\emptyset$ if $e=0$, all $A$ if $e>0$):
\begin{enumerate}[(E1)]
\item for every $j\notin A$ and every syndrome, some class that is ML-optimal for $(A,p)$ is also ML-optimal when qubit $j$ is erased in addition (``flagging one more qubit never changes the ML decision''); and
\item $\min_{D\in\mathcal D^\ast_A}\sum_{j\notin A}\partial_{q_j}L_A(p;D)=\sum_{j\notin A}\min_{D\in\mathcal D^\ast_A}\partial_{q_j}L_A(p;D)$, which holds automatically if the ML class is unique for every syndrome.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, at $e_0=0$ and a rate $p$ without ML ties, $R^{(d)}=c$ if and only if flagging any single qubit leaves an ML decision optimal.\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Equality in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:rate-right} holds iff it holds in (2) for every $(A,j)$ with $\pi_A>0$ and in (3) for every such $A$; the latter is (E2).
In (2), a concave function meets its right tangent at $\tfrac34$ iff it is affine on $[p,\tfrac34]$. Now $f_{A,j}=1-\sum_sg_s$ with $g_s=\max_cW_c$ convex; a sum of convex functions is affine iff each summand is.
Finally $g_s$ is affine on $[p,\tfrac34]$ iff some $c$ is maximal at both endpoints: if $W_c$ is maximal at both ends, every $W_{c'}-W_c$ is affine and $\le0$ at both ends, so $g_s=W_c$; conversely, if $g_s$ is affine, a class $c$ maximal at the midpoint satisfies $W_c\le g_s$ with equality at an interior point, so $W_c=g_s$ on the whole segment.
At $q_j=p$ maximality means ML-optimal for $(A,p)$, and at $q_j=\tfrac34$ it means ML-optimal for $A\cup\{j\}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:concave}(b)); this is (E1).
\end{proof}

\noindent Theorem~\ref{thm:equality} answers the first half of open problem O6: the bound is attained exactly when the ML decision is insensitive to one additional flag. The criterion is a finite statement about ML decisions at the rate $p$; \texttt{checks/sharp\_codes\_scan.py} tests the equivalence (E1)$\Leftrightarrow R^{(d)}=c$ at $e_0=0$ on a library of small codes.

\begin{proposition}[{The five-qubit code attains the bound at every rate}]\label{prop:five-sharp}
For $[[5,1,3]]$ at $e_0=0$ and every $0<p<\tfrac34$, the ML class is unique for every syndrome and (E1) holds. Hence $R^{(d)}(p,0)=c(p,0)=\tfrac34-p$ exactly.
For the $d=3$ rotated surface code (E1) fails (at $p=10^{-3},0.01,0.05,0.2$), so $R^{(d)}<c$ there. For the Steane code (E1) holds at these rates, but the ML class is not unique at $42$ of its $64$ syndromes and the tie condition (E2) fails, so again $R^{(d)}<c$ (numerically $R^{(d)}/c\to0.857$ as $p\to0$).\hfill\st{PROVED} (computer-assisted) for $[[5,1,3]]$; \st{NUMERICAL} for the other two codes
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $r=p/(3(1-p))\in(0,1)$. The weight of a class $c$ is proportional to the integer polynomial $W_c(r)=\sum_{E\in c}r^{\wt(E)}$, and, with qubit $j$ erased, to $W^{(j)}_c(r)=\sum_{E\in c}r^{\wt(E\text{ off }j)}$.
For each of the $16$ syndromes let $c_s$ be the class that is ML at small $r$. Every difference $\Delta(r)=W_{c_s}(r)-W_c(r)$ ($c\ne c_s$ in the same syndrome) and $\Delta^{(j)}(r)=W^{(j)}_{c_s}(r)-W^{(j)}_c(r)$ has degree $\le5$.
Substituting $r=1/(1+x)$ and multiplying by $(1+x)^5$ gives polynomials in $x$ whose coefficients are nonnegative integers, not all zero in the unflagged case. They are therefore positive (respectively nonnegative) for $x>0$, that is, for $r\in(0,1)$.
Hence $c_s$ is the unique ML class for $0<p<\tfrac34$ and stays ML-optimal under every single flag; Theorem~\ref{thm:equality} applies.
The finite computation is check K5 of \texttt{checks/completion\_checks.py} (exact integer arithmetic); the same check (tie-aware) finds a violation of (E1) for the $d=3$ surface code, and for Steane finds that (E1) holds while the ML class is not unique; the exact rates of \texttt{checks/sharp\_codes\_scan.py} show $R^{(d)}<c$ for both. (An earlier version of K5 ignored ties and wrongly reported a violation of (E1) for Steane.) The strict inequality is consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:equality}, consistent with the values of $R^{(d)}/c$ in Observation~\ref{obs:sharp}.
\end{proof}

\noindent What remains of O6 is the second half: a structural description of the codes satisfying (E1) (perfect codes? transitive codes?). $[[5,1,3]]$ is perfect, but perfection alone does not obviously imply (E1): a weight-$3$ logical through the flagged qubit can tie with the weight-$1$ correction at leading order, so higher orders decide.

\subsection{Which codes attain the bound (O6), and the gap problem}\label{sec:o6-gap}

Throughout, $e_0=0$ and $r=p/(3(1-p))\in(0,1)$ for $p\in(0,\tfrac34)$. For a class $c$ (a coset of $S$ in a syndrome) put $W_c(r)=\sum_{E\in c}r^{\wt(E)}$ and, for a qubit $j$, $W^{(j)}_c(r)=\sum_{E\in c}r^{\wt(E\text{ off }j)}$, where $\wt(E\text{ off }j)$ counts the non-identity positions of $E$ other than $j$.

\begin{proposition}[Criterion (E1) in polynomial form]\label{prop:lex}
At $e_0=0$, condition (E1) of Theorem~\ref{thm:equality} holds at the rate $p$ iff for every syndrome $s$ and every qubit $j$, $\arg\max_cW_c(r)\cap\arg\max_cW^{(j)}_c(r)\ne\emptyset$ (maxima over the classes of $s$). It holds for all sufficiently small $p$ iff the same is true with ``$\arg\max$'' taken in the lexicographic order of the coefficient sequences $(a_0,a_1,\dots)$ of the polynomials, read from the lowest power up.\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\Prob[\text{class }c]=(1-p)^nW_c(r)$; with qubit $j$ uniform (erased), $\Prob[\text{class }c]=\tfrac14(1-p)^{n-1}W^{(j)}_c(r)$. The common factors do not affect the maximizers. For polynomials $f\ne g$, $f(r)>g(r)$ for all small $r>0$ iff the coefficient sequence of $f$ is lexicographically larger.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Split-logical syndromes]\label{lem:split}
Let the code have $k\ge1$ and distance $d$, and let $G\in N\setminus S$ have weight $d$. Split $G=E_1E_2$ into Paulis with disjoint supports, $\wt E_1=\lfloor d/2\rfloor$, $\wt E_2=\lceil d/2\rceil$. Then $E_1,E_2$ have the same syndrome, lie in different classes $c_1,c_2$, and the minimum weights of these classes are exactly $\lfloor d/2\rfloor$ and $\lceil d/2\rceil$. Consequently every code has a syndrome at which two classes have minimum weights differing by at most one, and flagging one qubit of $\supp E_2$ makes their flagged minimum weights equal ($d$ odd) or reverses their order ($d$ even).\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\sigma(E_1)+\sigma(E_2)=\sigma(G)=0$ and $E_1E_2=G\notin S$. For $F_i\in c_i$, $F_1F_2\in GS\subseteq N\setminus S$, so $\wt F_1+\wt F_2\ge d$; with $\min_{c_i}\wt\le\wt E_i$ this forces equality.
\end{proof}

\noindent So (E1) is never decided by minimum weights alone; it is decided by multiplicities, as in the next corollary, or by higher orders, as for $[[5,1,3]]$ below.

\begin{corollary}[A first-order obstruction]\label{cor:first-order-e1}
Let $s$ be a syndrome with a unique class $c^\ast$ of minimum weight $w$, let $j$ be a qubit contained in no weight-$w$ element of $c^\ast$, and let $c'\ne c^\ast$ be a class of $s$ of minimum weight $w+1$ with
\[
\#\{E\in c':\wt E=w+1,\ j\in\supp E\}\;>\;\#\{E\in c^\ast:\wt E=w\}+\#\{E\in c^\ast:\wt E=w+1,\ j\in\supp E\}.
\]
Then (E1) fails for all sufficiently small $p$, and $R^{(d)}<c$ there.\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{corollary}
\noindent On $160$ random $[[n,1]]$ codes ($n=4$--$7$), (E1) fails at small $p$ for $138$, and the obstruction of Corollary~\ref{cor:first-order-e1} detects $113$ of them (check L1 of \texttt{checks/second\_pass\_checks.py}, which also confirms that every detected obstruction is a failure of the lexicographic criterion).
\begin{proof}
Unflagged, $c^\ast$ is the unique lexicographic maximum. Flagged at $j$, both $W^{(j)}_{c^\ast}$ and $W^{(j)}_{c'}$ start at $r^w$ (no element of $c^\ast$ has flagged weight below $w$), with the two coefficients displayed; so $c^\ast$ is not a lexicographic maximum after the flag. Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:lex} and Theorem~\ref{thm:equality}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The five-qubit code: a proof by hand]\label{prop:five-hand}
For $[[5,1,3]]$ the class polynomials are, for every qubit $k$, Pauli $P_k$, nontrivial logical class $c'$ and qubit $j\ne k$:
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
\text{syndrome }0: & W_S=1+15r^4, & W_{c'}=10r^3+6r^5,\\
& W^{(j)}_S=1+12r^3+3r^4, & W^{(j)}_{c'}=6r^2+4r^3+6r^4,\\
\text{syndrome }\sigma(P_k): & W_{c^\ast}=r+4r^3+8r^4+3r^5, & W_{c'}=2r^2+4r^3+6r^4+4r^5,\\
& W^{(k)}_{c^\ast}=1+12r^3+3r^4, & W^{(k)}_{c'}=6r^2+4r^3+6r^4,\\
& W^{(j)}_{c^\ast}=r+3r^2+7r^3+5r^4, & W^{(j)}_{c'}=r+3r^2+7r^3+5r^4,
\end{array}
\]
with $c^\ast=P_kS$. The differences are $W_S-W_{c'}=(1-r)^3(1+3r+6r^2)$, $W_{c^\ast}-W_{c'}=r(1-r)^3(1+r)$, $W^{(\cdot)}_{S}-W^{(\cdot)}_{c'}=W^{(k)}_{c^\ast}-W^{(k)}_{c'}=(1-r)^3(1+3r)$, and $W^{(j)}_{c^\ast}-W^{(j)}_{c'}\equiv0$.
Hence for every $p\in(0,\tfrac34)$ the ML class is unique and stays ML-optimal under any single flag, (E1) and (E2) hold, and $R^{(d)}(p,0)=c$. This proves Proposition~\ref{prop:five-sharp} without computer assistance.\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Facts used. (a) $S$ is generated by the cyclic shifts of $XZZXI$; its $15$ nontrivial elements have weight $4$, and the weight distribution of $N\setminus S$ is $30x^3+18x^5$ (MacWilliams, Corollary~\ref{cor:product}). (b) The cyclic shift and the transversal map $X\to Y\to Z\to X$ preserve $S$ (the latter maps $XZZXI$ to $YXXYI$, a cyclic shift of $(ZXIXZ)(ZZXIX)$ up to phase); the shift fixes each logical class and the transversal map permutes the three nontrivial classes, so each class has $10$ elements of weight $3$ and $6$ of weight $5$, and each qubit lies in the support of $3$ stabilizers' complements, i.e.\ $3$ stabilizers miss each qubit. (c) For a qubit $m$, the stabilizers missing $m$ form with $I$ a subgroup of order $4$ whose nontrivial elements have weight $4$, so at every other qubit they take the values $X,Y,Z$ once each. Restriction to qubit $k$ is a homomorphism $S\to\F^2$ with kernel of order $4$, so every value at $k$ (including $I$) occurs $4$ times in $S$, and hence $4$ times in every class $LS$. (d) Two distinct elements of one class differ by a stabilizer, so their product has weight $\ge4$.

Class $c'=P_kLS$ consists of $P_kL'$, $L'\in LS$. By (c), $L'_k=I$ for $4$ elements, which have weight $3$ (weight-$5$ elements have full support). For a value $Q\ne I$ at $k$, two weight-$3$ elements with $L'_k=Q$ have product of weight $\le4$ supported off $k$, so by (d) their other two qubits are complementary pairs of the four qubits $\ne k$; hence at most $2$ per value, and since $10-4=6$ weight-$3$ elements have $L'_k\ne I$, exactly $2$ per value, and then (c) gives exactly $2$ weight-$5$ elements per value. By (d) the four weight-$3$ elements with $L'_k=I$ have distinct supports, i.e.\ all four $3$-subsets of the qubits $\ne k$. Similarly in $c^\ast=P_kS$: $P_k$ itself; for each $m\ne k$ exactly one stabilizer with $s_k=P_k$ missing $m$ (by (c)), giving weight $3$ with support all but $\{k,m\}$; $8$ stabilizers with $s_k\notin\{I,P_k\}$, two missing each $m\ne k$, giving weight $4$; and $3$ stabilizers missing $k$, giving weight $5$.
Reading off the weights, and the weights off $j$ or off $k$ (a support contains $j$ unless $j$ is the missing qubit or lies in the complementary pair), gives the table; for syndrome $0$ use that $6$ of the $10$ weight-$3$ elements of a class contain a given qubit (cyclic symmetry) and $12$ of the $15$ stabilizers do. The factorizations are checked by expanding. All differences are $\ge0$ on $(0,1)$, strictly for the unflagged ones; apply Proposition~\ref{prop:lex}, which needs the exact criterion at every $p$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:equality}.
\end{proof}

\noindent\emph{Mechanism.} At a single-error syndrome, flagging any other qubit makes all four classes \emph{exactly} equally likely at every $p$: one erasure plus one unknown error exceed what a distance-$3$ code can resolve, and for this code the posterior of the logical becomes exactly uniform instead of merely ambiguous, so the flag cannot move the decision. The other flags leave the ML class strictly optimal. The exact uniformity looks like a consequence of the fact that the five-qubit code is a perfect tensor (every three of its six legs, five physical and one logical, are maximally mixed). \emph{Perfect-tensor codes} are answered for qubits by Observation~\ref{obs:ame-codes}: they satisfy (E1), but only $[[5,1,3]]$ also satisfies (E2). By Lemma~\ref{lem:split} the answer is never determined by the distance alone. The $d=3$ surface code violates (E1), and the Steane code satisfies (E1) but fails the tie condition (E2) (check K5 and Proposition~\ref{prop:five-sharp}), so neither being CSS nor being a surface code suffices; the Steane example shows that (E2) is not automatic for codes with exact ML ties.

\begin{proposition}[The gap is code dependence]\label{prop:gap-A}
Let $\succeq_{\rm univ}$ be the code-universal operational order on $\Nn_{\rm cc}$: $(p,e)\succeq_{\rm univ}(p',e')$ iff $\err_{\mathfrak D}(p',e')\ge\err_{\mathfrak D}(p,e)$ for every linear decoding structure $\mathfrak D$ on any number of qubits. Then the set of marginal rates, at $x_0=(p_0,e_0)$, of the monotones of $\succeq_{\rm univ}$ that have right partial derivatives there has supremum exactly $c(p_0,e_0)$, attained by the uncoded qubit's $\err=\tfrac34(1-\mu)$ and, at $e_0=0$, by $[[5,1,3]]$. The same holds for every order that contains the local free order and under which $\err$ of every structure is monotone (any ``code-universally sound'' enlargement of the free operations).
Hence no enlargement of the free operations that is sound for all codes can lower the bound $c$, whether or not Conjecture~\ref{conj:universal} holds, and a strict gap $c-R_\alpha>0$ for the surface code is a property of the surface code (case (A) of Problem~\ref{prob:gap}). Case (B) can occur only through operations that are sound for the surface code but not for all codes, i.e.\ through code dependence.\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\succeq_{\rm univ}$ contains the local free order (Theorem~\ref{thm:preorder}), so its monotones are monotones of the local free order and have rates in $[0,c]$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:marginal}(a)). The uncoded qubit's $\err=\tfrac34(1-\mu)$ is a monotone of $\succeq_{\rm univ}$ by definition, with rate $c$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:marginal}(b)); at $e_0=0$ so is $\err$ of $[[5,1,3]]$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:five-hand}). The same argument applies to any order with the two stated properties.
\end{proof}

\noindent What remains of the gap problem is quantitative: why the surface code sits at $R_\alpha\approx0.3c$ (the hypothesis $H_B$, Conjecture~\ref{conj:HB}).
\emph{Heuristic support for $H_B$ at small $B$ (not a proof).} At $p\to0$ ($e=0$) and at $e\to0$ ($p=0$) the dominant failures are, respectively, about half of a minimum-weight logical in error and a whole minimum-weight logical erased; both give $\err_d\approx N_d\,B^d$ up to factors $e^{o(d)}$, with $N_d$ the number of minimum-weight logicals, so $\alpha\approx\ln(1/B)-\lim\tfrac1d\ln N_d$ along both axes, a function of $B$ alone to leading order. A proof would need a lower bound on $\err_d$ that captures the entropy $\lim\tfrac1d\ln N_d$; the genie bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:genie} uses a single column and misses it.

\begin{observation}[Codes from qubit AME states]\label{obs:ame-codes}
Absolutely maximally entangled (AME) qubit states exist only on $2$, $3$, $5$ and $6$ qubits (a known classification; not re-derived here), and a perfect tensor is an AME state on an even number of legs. Splitting the stabilizer AME states on $3$, $5$ and $6$ qubits into $k\ge1$ input legs and the remaining physical legs gives, for every choice of the input legs, the codes $[[2,1,1]]$, $[[4,1,2]]$, $[[3,2,1]]$, $[[5,1,3]]$ and $[[4,2,2]]$. With exact ML at $e_0=0$ and $p\in\{10^{-3},0.01,0.05,0.2,0.5\}$:
\begin{itemize}
\item all of them satisfy (E1) at every tested $p$ (tie-aware, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:equality});
\item only $[[5,1,3]]$ has a unique ML class at every syndrome; the others have exact ML ties, (E2) fails, and $R^{(d)}<c$: $R^{(d)}/c\approx0.80,0.75,0$ at small $p$ for $[[2,1,1]]$, $[[3,2,1]]$ and $[[4,2,2]]$, and $R^{(d)}/c\approx0.001$--$0.31$ for $[[4,1,2]]$;
\item for comparison, Steane satisfies (E1) and fails (E2) ($R^{(d)}/c\to0.857$), and the $d=3$ surface code fails (E1) ($R^{(d)}/c\approx0.73$).
\end{itemize}
\hfill\st{NUMERICAL} (exact enumeration, check M8)
\end{observation}

\noindent So for qubits, ``perfect tensor'' explains (E1) on every available example, but equality in the exchange-rate bound needs (E2) as well, and among codes with $1\le k<n$ only $[[5,1,3]]$ has it. A proof that every AME code satisfies (E1), and the qudit case, are open; so is a classification of the codes that satisfy (E1) and (E2).
