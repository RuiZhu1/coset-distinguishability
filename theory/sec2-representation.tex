\section{How erasure, leakage, and event-type noise are represented in the model}\label{sec:repr}

Monotonicity (Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone}) holds only for superpositions that are ``classically samplable and independent of the hidden variables''
(Remark~\ref{rem:independence}). This section explains, for each physical noise type, how to write it as a noise instance $(E,F)$,
under which assumptions the monotonicity argument still holds, and where it fails.

\subsection{Erasure}

\begin{proposition}[Erasure = flagged completely depolarizing noise]\label{prop:erasure}
Consider a stabilizer code with ideal (noiseless) syndrome extraction. Qubit $j$ is erased and flagged ($F_j=1$) with probability $e$, after which it is in the maximally mixed state.
In the $(E,F)$ representation, erasure is then equivalent to: $F_j=1$ and $E_j$ uniform on $\{I,X,Y,Z\}$, independent of the other qubits.
\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Replacement by the maximally mixed state is the channel $\Lambda(\rho)=\operatorname{tr}(\rho)\,\mathbb 1/2=\tfrac14\sum_{P\in\{I,X,Y,Z\}}P\rho P$
(the Pauli-twirl identity). The overall channel is therefore a convex combination of Pauli unitaries; syndrome measurement is a linear function on the Pauli frame,
so the whole process is equivalent to ``random Pauli + classical flag''.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Conservativeness]
If an actual erasure resets the qubit to a fixed known state (such as $\lvert 0\rangle$) or leaves a leaked state, the experimenter can additionally apply a random Pauli to that location
(with a flag); this is again a free operation of type (F2$'$) and can only make the problem harder. Hence the ``maximally mixed model'' gives an
$\err$ that is an \emph{upper bound on the $\err$ of the real noise} (it errs on the conservative side), not a lower bound.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Discarding the flag = depolarizing]\label{cor:discard}
Discarding the flag (F1$'$) of an erased qubit yields an unflagged uniform Pauli error, that is, depolarizing noise of rate $3/4$ (the probability of a nontrivial Pauli is $3/4$).
\end{corollary}

\subsection{The code-capacity class $\Nn_{\rm cc}(p,e)$ and three operations on it}

\begin{definition}\label{def:Ncc}
$\Nn_{\rm cc}(p,e)$: each data qubit independently is erased with probability $e$ (flag $F_j=1$, $E_j$ uniform),
and otherwise suffers depolarizing noise with probability $p$ ($E_j=X,Y,Z$ each with probability $p/3$). Write $\lambda(p)=1-\tfrac43p$ (the Pauli eigenvalue of the depolarizing channel) and
$\mu(p,e)=(1-e)\lambda(p)$. Note that $\Dep_p$ is linear in $p$ and that $\Dep_{3/4}$ is the uniform distribution.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Basic free operations within the class]\label{lem:basic-ops}
The following operations map $\Nn_{\rm cc}(p,e)$ to $\Nn_{\rm cc}(p',e')$, and each is a special case of (F1$'$)/(F2)/(F2$'$):
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item \emph{Superposing depolarizing noise} (F2$'$, acting only on unflagged qubits): $p'=p\oplus q$, i.e.\ $\lambda(p')=\lambda(p)\lambda(q)$; $e'=e$.
\item \emph{Superposing erasure} (F2): $p'=p$, $e'=1-(1-e)(1-\delta)$.
\item \emph{Randomly discarding flags} (F1$'$): each flagged qubit independently drops its flag with probability $\kappa\in[0,1]$. Then
\[
e'=e(1-\kappa),\qquad p'=\frac{(1-e)p+\tfrac34 e\kappa}{1-e+e\kappa},\qquad
\lambda(p')=\frac{(1-e)\lambda(p)}{1-e+e\kappa}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(a) and (b) follow directly from the composition of depolarizing channels and the fact that ``erasure overwrites an existing fault'' (the sum of uniform noise and any independent noise is again uniform);
in (b) the newly erased qubits have $E$ made uniform and the flag is merged in.

(c) Each qubit is treated independently. After the flag is dropped: a qubit is still flagged with probability $e(1-\kappa)$, and then $E$ is uniform; an unflagged qubit comes, with probability
$(1-e)$, from a non-erased qubit (law $\Dep_p$) and, with probability $e\kappa$, from an erasure whose flag was dropped (law $\Dep_{3/4}$).
Given ``unflagged'', the law of $E$ is the mixture of $\Dep_p$ and $\Dep_{3/4}$ with weights $(1-e):e\kappa$;
since $\Dep_p$ is linear in $p$ the mixture is again $\Dep_{p'}$ with $p'$ as above; the qubits are independent, so the result is again in $\Nn_{\rm cc}$.
The formula for $\lambda$ follows from the linearity of $\lambda=1-\tfrac43p$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Leakage}

Physically, a leaked qubit leaves the computational subspace; during its lifetime it causes random Pauli errors in the gates that act on it and makes measurement results random.
We bring leakage into the model class through the following \emph{assumption}.

\begin{definition}[Assumption (L): leakage is classically samplable]\label{def:L}
The leakage process is described by a random \emph{set of leakage events} $\Lambda$ (locations, start times, durations $\le\tau$) such that
\begin{enumerate}[(L1)]
\item after twirling over the computational subspace, the entire effect of leakage on the detectors is equivalent to a fault vector $E_\Lambda\in V$ whose conditional law
$P(E_\Lambda\mid\Lambda)$ is known (typically: every gate involving a leaked qubit applies a uniform Pauli with probability $1/2$);
\item the law of $\Lambda$ is known and \emph{independent} of the background faults $E_{\rm bg}$;
\item the side information $F_\Lambda$ is a known (possibly partial) function of $\Lambda$: full detection corresponds to flagged events, no detection to $F_\Lambda=\text{constant}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Monotonicity under leakage]\label{prop:leak}
Under assumption (L) the total noise instance is $(E_{\rm bg}+E_\Lambda,\,(F_{\rm bg},F_\Lambda))$, and $(E_\Lambda,F_\Lambda)$ is a random pair independent of the background and
classically samplable. Hence Proposition~\ref{prop:free-ops} applies:\\
(1) adding leakage does not decrease $\err$;\\
(2) monotonicity in the leakage event rate $r_\ell$: a Poisson leakage process of rate $r_\ell+r'_\ell$ equals the sum of a process of rate $r_\ell$ and an independent process of rate $r'_\ell$
(Poisson superposition theorem; faults add in $\F$), so $\err$ is nondecreasing in $r_\ell$;\\
(3) discarding the leakage-detection flags is (F1$'$) and does not decrease $\err$.\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Outside assumption (L): where the argument fails]\label{rem:L-fail}
If (L2) fails, the superposed layer depends on a hidden variable, the processor cannot sample it independently (Remark~\ref{rem:independence}), and the monotonicity argument breaks down. Typical cases:
(i) the leakage probability depends on the computational basis state of the qubit (for example $\lvert1\rangle$ leaks more easily), which correlates $\Lambda$ with the background faults;
(ii) the action of a leaked qubit on its neighbors is a coherent rotation rather than a random Pauli, so the twirling approximation in (L1) fails;
(iii) leakage is coupled to measurement/reset errors through the same physical mechanism. These are listed as \emph{model-class assumptions} and lie outside the proofs of these notes,
consistently with the ``Scope and limitations'' of the abstract: coherent errors are outside the model class.
The approximation in (L1) that the twirled leakage noise is Pauli is itself an experimentally testable assumption and should be listed together with coherent errors among the model-class assumptions.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Local burst events and chip-scale events}

\begin{definition}[Additive event layers]\label{def:events}
Let the background $(E_{\rm bg},F_{\rm bg})$ be as above. A \emph{local burst layer}: a Poisson point process in space-time with intensity $r\,\mu(dz\,dt)$;
each event (center $z$, start $t$) independently applies depolarizing noise of rate $p_b$ at every fault location in a space-time region of radius $R$ and duration $T_b$.
A \emph{chip-scale layer}: Poisson events of rate $r_c$; each event applies some known fault law $K_c$ to all locations of the chip for a duration $\tau_c$.
All layers are independent of each other and of the background, and faults add in $\F$ (\emph{additive semantics}, consistent with the ``detection events are XOR-superposed'' remark in README P2).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Monotonicity in the rate directions]\label{prop:rate-mono}
In the model class of Definition~\ref{def:events}, $\err$ is nondecreasing in $p$, $e$, the burst rate $r$, and the chip-event rate $r_c$.
\hfill\st{PROVED}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A Poisson process of intensity $r+r'$ is the union of independent processes of intensities $r$ and $r'$ (superposition theorem), and under additive semantics the faults of the events add in $\F$,
so increasing $r$ is exactly the superposition of an independent, classically samplable burst layer; the same holds for $r_c$, $p$, and $e$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:basic-ops}).
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:free-ops}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Monotonicity in the shape parameters $(R,T_b,p_b,\tau_c)$ is \emph{not} given by free operations]\label{rem:shape}
Increasing $p_b$ (or $R$, $T_b$) amounts to superposing further noise \emph{inside the burst regions that have occurred}. When burst events are invisible to the decoder (unflagged),
this additional layer depends on a hidden variable (the event locations), the processor cannot sample it independently, and the argument fails.
If burst events are \emph{flagged} (the locations and times of events enter $F$), the additional layer depends on the observed $F$, which is of type (F2$'$), and monotonicity holds.
For unflagged bursts, monotonicity in the shape parameters \emph{fails} in general: Proposition~\ref{prob:O1} gives an exact counterexample in the strength $p_b$ on the $d=3$ surface code (open problem O1, answered negatively).
The certification theorem does not need it: Theorem~\ref{thm:burst-cert} is uniform over burst strengths (\S\ref{sec:cert}).
\end{remark}
