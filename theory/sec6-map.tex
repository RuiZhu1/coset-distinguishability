\section{Theory--numerics map and open problems}\label{sec:map}

Principle: \textbf{every theorem comes with a numerical falsification test}. A passing test does not prove the theorem, but a violation shows that the theorem (or the implementation) is wrong;
conversely, numerical results decide which statement to prove next.

\paragraph{A second use of the exact small-code computation.}
\texttt{exact\_small\_codes.py} gives the \emph{exact} maximum-likelihood $\err(p,e)$ for codes with $n\le9$, and serves directly as the oracle for M1 (the ML decoder):
on the $d=3$ rotated surface code the ML decoder of \texttt{lcd.decoders.ExactTNMLDecoder} must agree with it to machine precision, which is a stronger gate than the sanity checks in the README.
This gate has been passed (\texttt{tests/test\_tn\_ml.py} compares coset sums with brute-force enumeration to $10^{-12}$, and \texttt{experiments/p1\_erasure\_pauli/crosscheck\_ml.py} compares the stratified ML estimate with erasures against the oracle).

\paragraph{Division of tests: MWPM cannot falsify upper bounds for ML.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:union}, Theorem~\ref{thm:cc-cert}, and similar statements are upper bounds for ML, while the MWPM failure rate is $\ge\err$,
so MWPM data can neither confirm nor refute these bounds; one must use exact ML (small $d$) or tensor-network ML.
Lower bounds (the floor, the Poisson lower bound, the genie lower bound) hold for any decoder and can be tested with stim + MWPM.

\subsection{List of open problems}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The gap problem} (Problem~\ref{prob:gap}): \emph{qualitative part settled}: no code-universally sound enlargement of the free operations lowers the bound $c$, so the gap is code dependence (Proposition~\ref{prop:gap-A}). Open: the quantitative value $R_\alpha\approx0.3c$ of the surface code, i.e.\ hypothesis $H_B$ (Conjecture~\ref{conj:HB}; heuristic support at small $B$ in \S\ref{sec:o6-gap}).
\item \textbf{Single-loss form of Conjecture~\ref{conj:universal}} (Remark~\ref{rem:conj-reform}, \S\ref{sec:single-loss}): does the code-universal order of $\err=1-\Phi_{\max}$ alone coincide with the local free order? The all-losses form is proved (Proposition~\ref{prop:local-level1}); the single-loss form is proved on $\{\mu'>\mu\}\cup\{\nu'>\nu\}$ and the search gives evidence against it on the trade region. \emph{To settle it:} a proof of non-reversal for all $n$, or structures with $\err_A\approx p$ for all $|A|<k$ for every $k$. 
  \emph{Prerequisite numerics}: counterexample search over a library of stabilizer codes; for orders of the surface code, the nesting $\succeq_{\rm local}\subseteq\succeq_{\mathrm{L1}}\subseteq\succeq_{\err}$.
\item \textbf{Asymptotic rates in the tensor-power sense} (R-c of \S\ref{sec:rates}): necessary conditions proved (multiplicative $\Phi_\alpha$, additive $H(L\mid Y)$; Proposition~\ref{prop:hash}). Open: sufficiency for large $n$ or with catalysts, in particular for trade pairs, and a rule for changing the label alphabet ($m\ne n$).
\item \textbf{O1} (Proposition~\ref{prob:O1}): \emph{answered negatively}: $\err$ is not monotone in the burst strength $p_b$ (exact counterexample on the $d=3$ surface code). Not needed for certification (Theorem~\ref{thm:burst-cert}). The radius and duration directions are untested.
\item \textbf{O2} (Lemma~\ref{lem:robust}, Proposition~\ref{prop:no-rate-limit}): \emph{partly settled}. Limits for $p$ and $e$ are robust to contamination; limits for burst rates need a detection efficiency, and without a strength floor no uniformly valid limit exists. Space-time version: Lemma~\ref{lem:st-robust}. Open: a certificate uniform over weak bursts, and a certified lower bound on the detection efficiency.
\item \textbf{O3} (Theorem~\ref{thm:burst-product}): \emph{settled at code capacity}: a single-sum product bound $\tfrac12\widetilde W(B_{\rm burst})$, with no floor; for any linear decoding structure (circuit level) by Theorems~\ref{thm:st-burst}, \ref{thm:st-cert}. Open: computing $\widetilde W^{\mathcal L}$ for a circuit-level detector error model.
\item \textbf{O4} (Problem~\ref{prob:O4}): \emph{two-hypothesis part settled under the marking model}: exact pattern gain $D_{\rm pattern}=D_{\rm count}+\Lambda_1D(\pi_1\|\pi_0)$, Stein form of the ratio of rounds, and adaptive designs do not beat the best single experiment (Theorems~\ref{thm:pattern-gain}--\ref{thm:adaptive}). Open: composite hypotheses, the adaptive strong converse and Chernoff exponent, overlapping events, and $D_{\rm pattern}(d)$ for the surface code.
\item \textbf{O5} (Theorem~\ref{thm:mwpm-peierls}, Corollary~\ref{cor:mwpm-cert}): \emph{settled for code-capacity MWPM with erasures} by a Peierls bound; crude constants. Open: phenomenological and circuit-level MWPM, and sharper constants.
\item \textbf{O6} (Theorem~\ref{thm:equality}, Proposition~\ref{prop:five-sharp}): \emph{first half settled}: equality in the rate bound holds iff one extra flag never changes the ML decision (plus a tie condition); proved for $[[5,1,3]]$ at all $p$, now also by hand (Proposition~\ref{prop:five-hand}: one flag at a single-error syndrome makes the four classes exactly equally likely); small-$p$ criterion and first-order obstruction (Proposition~\ref{prop:lex}, Corollary~\ref{cor:first-order-e1}); minimum weights never decide it (Lemma~\ref{lem:split}). Open: whether all perfect-tensor codes satisfy it, and other families.
\item \textbf{O7}: tightening the union bound so that certification is no longer vacuous at realistic parameters. \emph{Partial}: a closed-form ML certificate for every $d$ via MWPM (Proposition~\ref{prop:ml-peierls}) and a self-avoiding-walk refinement extending its range from $4.29\%$ to about $4.9\%$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:saw}). Open: anything close to the ML threshold $18.9\%$.
\end{enumerate}
