\section{Theory--numerics map and open problems}\label{sec:map}

Principle: \textbf{every theorem comes with a numerical falsification test}. A passing test does not prove the theorem, but a violation shows that the theorem (or the implementation) is wrong;
conversely, numerical results decide which statement to prove next.

\paragraph{A second use of the exact small-code computation.}
\texttt{exact\_small\_codes.py} gives the \emph{exact} maximum-likelihood $\err(p,e)$ for codes with $n\le9$, and serves directly as the oracle for M1 (the ML decoder):
on the $d=3$ rotated surface code the ML decoder of \texttt{lcd.decoders.ExactTNMLDecoder} must agree with it to machine precision, which is a stronger gate than the sanity checks in the README.
This gate has been passed (\texttt{tests/test\_tn\_ml.py} compares coset sums with brute-force enumeration to $10^{-12}$, and \texttt{experiments/p1\_erasure\_pauli/crosscheck\_ml.py} compares the stratified ML estimate with erasures against the oracle).

\paragraph{Division of tests: MWPM cannot falsify upper bounds for ML.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:union}, Theorem~\ref{thm:cc-cert}, and similar statements are upper bounds for ML, while the MWPM failure rate is $\ge\err$,
so MWPM data can neither confirm nor refute these bounds; one must use exact ML (small $d$) or tensor-network ML.
Lower bounds (the floor, the Poisson lower bound, the genie lower bound) hold for any decoder and can be tested with stim + MWPM.

\subsection{List of open problems}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The gap problem} (Problem~\ref{prob:gap}): does $c-R_\alpha$ come from code dependence or from incompleteness of the free operations?
  \emph{Prerequisite numerics}: the $R_\alpha$ of P1; \emph{theoretical route}: the Blackwell/R\'enyi route of Conjecture~\ref{conj:universal}.
\item \textbf{O1} (Problem~\ref{prob:O1}): monotonicity of $\err$ in the shape parameters $p_b,R,T_b$ for unflagged bursts.
\item \textbf{O2} (Problem~\ref{prob:O2}): confidence upper limits for the parameters under contamination by burst/chip events.
\item \textbf{O3} (Problem~\ref{prob:O3}): explicit estimates for the union bound for burst mixtures (cluster expansion).
\item \textbf{O4} (Problem~\ref{prob:O4}): the experimental-design theorem.
\item \textbf{O5}: certification for \emph{suboptimal decoders} (MWPM): this needs a Peierls-type upper bound for that decoder; the bounds of these notes are for ML only.
\item \textbf{O6}: a proof of Observation~\ref{obs:sharp}: for which classes of codes (perfect codes, transitive codes) does flagging a single qubit leave the ML decision unchanged?
\item \textbf{O7}: tightening the union bound (calibrating with measured small-$d$ failure rates) so that certification is no longer vacuous at realistic parameters.
\end{enumerate}
