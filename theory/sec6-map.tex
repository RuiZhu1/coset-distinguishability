\section{Theory--numerics map and open problems}\label{sec:map}

Principle: \textbf{every theorem comes with a numerical falsification test}. A passing test does not prove the theorem, but a violation shows that the theorem (or the implementation) is wrong;
conversely, numerical results decide which statement to prove next.

\paragraph{A second use of the exact small-code computation.}
\texttt{exact\_small\_codes.py} gives the \emph{exact} maximum-likelihood $\err(p,e)$ for codes with $n\le9$, and serves directly as the oracle for M1 (the ML decoder):
on the $d=3$ rotated surface code the ML decoder of \texttt{lcd.decoders.ExactTNMLDecoder} must agree with it to machine precision, which is a stronger gate than the sanity checks in the README.
This gate has been passed (\texttt{tests/test\_tn\_ml.py} compares coset sums with brute-force enumeration to $10^{-12}$, and \texttt{experiments/p1\_erasure\_pauli/crosscheck\_ml.py} compares the stratified ML estimate with erasures against the oracle).

\paragraph{Division of tests: MWPM cannot falsify upper bounds for ML.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:union}, Theorem~\ref{thm:cc-cert}, and similar statements are upper bounds for ML, while the MWPM failure rate is $\ge\err$,
so MWPM data can neither confirm nor refute these bounds; one must use exact ML (small $d$) or tensor-network ML.
Lower bounds (the floor, the Poisson lower bound, the genie lower bound) hold for any decoder and can be tested with stim + MWPM.

\subsection{List of open problems}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The gap problem} (Problem~\ref{prob:gap}): \emph{qualitative part settled}: no code-universally sound enlargement of the free operations lowers the bound $c$, so the gap is code dependence (Proposition~\ref{prop:gap-A}). Open: the quantitative value $R_\alpha\approx0.3c$ of the surface code, i.e.\ hypothesis $H_B$ (Conjecture~\ref{conj:HB}; heuristic support at small $B$ in \S\ref{sec:o6-gap}).
\item \textbf{Single-loss form of Conjecture~\ref{conj:universal}} (Remark~\ref{rem:conj-reform}, \S\ref{sec:single-loss}): does the code-universal order of $\err=1-\Phi_{\max}$ alone coincide with the local free order? The all-losses form is proved (Proposition~\ref{prop:local-level1}); the single-loss form is proved on $\{\mu'>\mu\}\cup\{\nu'>\nu\}$ and the search gives evidence against it on the trade region. \emph{To settle it:} a proof of non-reversal for all $n$, or structures with $\err_A\approx p$ for all $|A|<k$ for every $k$. 
  \emph{Prerequisite numerics}: counterexample search over a library of stabilizer codes; for orders of the surface code, the nesting $\succeq_{\rm local}\subseteq\succeq_{\mathrm{L1}}\subseteq\succeq_{\err}$.
\item \textbf{Asymptotic rates in the tensor-power sense} (R-c of \S\ref{sec:rates}): \emph{settled for the uncoded qubit}: exact and catalytic conversion give back the one-shot order (Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-tensor}); approximate conversion is decided by $H(L\mid Y)$, with rate $N(P)/N(Q)$ and marginal rate $R_{\rm hash}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:approx-tensor}); trade pairs are reachable approximately but not exactly. Open: coded structures.
\item \textbf{O1} (Proposition~\ref{prob:O1}, Observations~\ref{obs:O1-region}, \ref{obs:O1-duration}): \emph{answered negatively in all three shape directions}: $\err$ can decrease when the burst strength, the region or the duration grows (exact counterexamples). Not needed for certification. Along balls of growing radius no decrease was found on $d=3$.
\item \textbf{O2} (Lemma~\ref{lem:robust}, Proposition~\ref{prop:no-rate-limit}, Theorem~\ref{thm:weak-bursts}): \emph{settled at code capacity}: limits for $p$ and $e$ are robust; the total burst rate cannot be bounded without a strength floor, but a certificate needs none: strong events are bounded with a certified efficiency and weak events are absorbed into the background estimate. At circuit level the certificate follows from Corollary~\ref{cor:circuit-bursts} under (H1)--(H2), which are checked for $d\le15$. Open: weak chip-scale events.
\item \textbf{O3} (Theorems~\ref{thm:burst-product}, \ref{thm:peierls-bursts}): \emph{settled at code capacity}: bursts in regions of bounded size give no $d$-independent floor (Peierls route, Theorem~\ref{thm:peierls-bursts}). The single-sum bound $\tfrac12\widetilde W(B_{\rm burst})$ holds at every $d$ but is vacuous as $d\to\infty$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:lower}(b)); an earlier version drew the no-floor conclusion from it. At circuit level: no floor under (H1)--(H2) (Corollary~\ref{cor:circuit-bursts}; the space-time union bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:st-burst} has the same degeneracy problem and is not used). Open: a check of (H1)--(H2) beyond $d=15$, or a proof from the structure of the circuit.
\item \textbf{O4} (Problem~\ref{prob:O4}): \emph{mostly settled under the marking model}: exact pattern gain, Stein ratio of rounds, adaptivity does not help (weak and strong converse, Theorems~\ref{thm:adaptive}, \ref{thm:adaptive-strong}); for composite alternatives in the detection orientation the optimal exponent is $\max_w\min_\theta\sum_iw_iD_i(\theta)/c_i$, attained non-adaptively (Theorem~\ref{thm:composite}). Open: the false-alarm orientation with composite alternatives (bounds in Proposition~\ref{prop:composite-fa}), the adaptive Chernoff exponent, overlapping events, and $D_{\rm pattern}(d)$ for the surface code.
\item \textbf{O5} (Theorem~\ref{thm:mwpm-peierls}, Corollary~\ref{cor:mwpm-cert}): \emph{settled for code-capacity MWPM with erasures} by a Peierls bound. \emph{Settled at circuit level and for phenomenological noise} for stim's rotated memory circuit (Theorem~\ref{thm:circuit-peierls}, Propositions~\ref{prop:circuit-numbers}, \ref{prop:circuit-lattice}): computable rigorous bounds, $24$--$1700$ times the simulated failure rate, and a certified suppression factor $\Lambda\ge3.8$ at $p=10^{-3}$. The constants are crude. Open: sharper constants (the Peierls step, not the counting, loses the factor), and erasure flags at circuit level.
\item \textbf{O6} (Theorem~\ref{thm:equality}, Propositions~\ref{prop:five-sharp}, \ref{prop:five-hand}, Observation~\ref{obs:ame-codes}): equality holds iff (E1) and (E2); proved for $[[5,1,3]]$ by hand; minimum weights never decide (E1) (Lemma~\ref{lem:split}). For qubits, all codes from AME states satisfy (E1) but only $[[5,1,3]]$ satisfies (E2). Open: a proof that AME codes satisfy (E1) in general (qudits), and a classification of the codes with (E1) and (E2).
\item \textbf{O7}: a certificate that is not vacuous at realistic parameters and large $d$. \emph{Partial}: the union bound is useless as $d\to\infty$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:lower}(b)); the Peierls bounds (Propositions~\ref{prop:ml-peierls}, \ref{prop:saw}, Lemma~\ref{lem:row-count}) are valid for all $d$ but only up to $p\approx5\%$. At circuit level, Proposition~\ref{prop:circuit-lattice} is valid for every $d$ up to $p\approx1.4\times10^{-3}$, which includes the usual benchmark point $p=10^{-3}$, but is still far from the threshold. Open: anything close to the ML threshold $18.9\%$ (code capacity) or to the circuit-level threshold.
\item \textbf{$H_B$} (Conjecture~\ref{conj:HB}, \S\ref{sec:hb}): \emph{leading order proved near the erasure axis} ($\alpha=\ln\frac1B-\ln2+O(B)$ at $p=0$, Theorem~\ref{thm:erasure-exponent}; Proposition~\ref{prop:hb-status}). Open at $e=0$: an ML lower bound with path entropy, and a Peierls bound with the per-qubit factor $B$.
\end{enumerate}
